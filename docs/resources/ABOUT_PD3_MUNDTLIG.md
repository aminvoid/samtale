## 1. Quick facts (2026)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Level** | B2 (CEFR). PD3 is the final exam after module 5 of Danskuddannelse 3. The *Studieprøven* comes after module 6. |
| **Format** | Individual (*individuelt*). You, an examiner (*eksaminator*) and a censor (*censor*) sit at one table. |
| **Total time** | **15 minutes.** This includes the ID check, instructions, the examiner and censor deciding your grade (*votering*), and being told your grade. |
| **Delprøve 1** | Your **prepared topic**: a presentation of about **2 min**, then follow-up questions for about **2½ min**. About 5 min in total, including instructions. |
| **Delprøve 2** | An **unprepared topic**. You draw 1 of 3 topics by lot. You get about **10 seconds** to look at two pictures, then answer questions for about **4½ min**. About 5 min in total. |
| **Your topic for delprøve 1** | Chosen by the examiner (a teacher at your school) and approved by the head of the school. You get it **5 weekdays before the oral period**: this term, **24 November 2026**. |
| **What you may bring** | Your own notes, **no more than one A4 page**, for the presentation. |
| **Grade** | **One grade** for oral communication on the 7-point scale. You hear it **right after the exam**. |
| **Weight** | The oral grade **counts double** in the PD3 average. |
| **Passing PD3** | The average of reading, writing and oral (oral counted twice) must be **at least 2.0**. |
| **Listening** | There is no separate listening test. Listening (*lytteforståelse*) is part of the oral grade. |

## 3. The oral exam in 2026, step by step

### 3.1 Who is in the room

- **Examiner (*eksaminator*):** a teacher at the school holding the exam. The examiner explains each part, asks all the questions and keeps the conversation on the topic. The rules say it should preferably **not be your own teacher** (§ 39).
- **Censor (*censor*):** a teacher from **another** language centre. Censors may never work where they are grading, and some are appointed by SIRI (*beskikket censor*). The censor checks your ID, takes notes, keeps time and can ask extra questions if needed. During the grading the censor leads and proposes a grade first.
- **Public, but no recording:** the exam is formally public (*offentlig*), but the head of the school can limit this. **Audio and video recording is not allowed.**

### 3.2 The 15 minutes, in order

| Step | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Start | short | The censor checks your ID. You, the examiner and the censor introduce yourselves. |
| **Delprøve 1: presentation** | ~2 min | The examiner explains the task and says: *"Værsgo, vil du godt præsentere dit emne."* You present **without interruption**. |
| **Delprøve 1: follow-up questions** | ~2½ min | The examiner asks questions about your presentation and ends with *"tak"*. |
| **Delprøve 2: draw a topic** | ~10 sec | You draw topic A, B or C. A sheet with two pictures is put in front of you, and you have about 10 seconds to look at it. |
| **Delprøve 2: questions** | ~4½ min | Two required questions, each with follow-ups. The examiner ends with: *"Tak, så er prøven forbi."* |
| Grading (*votering*) | short | You wait outside. Only the examiner and censor may be in the room. |
| Grade | short | You come back in. The examiner tells you your grade, with the censor present. |

Under the rules, your oral exam has officially **started the moment your presentation starts** (§ 26).

### 3.3 Delprøve 1: your prepared topic (*forberedt emne*)

#### The topic

- **Who chooses:** your teacher (the examiner) picks the topic, normally from the topics you worked on in **module 5**, and the head of the school approves it. **You cannot choose or request a topic.** For self-study candidates the head of the school may choose instead.
- **Spread:** in a class, the school tries to give everyone **different** topics. The topic is not drawn at random, because things like age are taken into account. For example, the guide says not to give a very young man "child-rearing in Denmark".
- **Type of topic:** it must be a **general, society-level topic** (*alment emne på et generelt niveau*) about a societal issue (*samfundsmæssig problematik*). It is usually written as *"X med fokus på Y"*.

SIRI's guide on choosing topics (January 2025) gives these examples of good PD3 topics:

> - Christiania med fokus på stedets rolle som alternativ boform
> - Danskernes alkoholforbrug med fokus på unges alkoholvaner
> - Danske unges økonomi med fokus på gæld og overforbrug
> - Danske unges medieforbrug med fokus på sociale medier
> - Livsstilssygdomme med fokus på diabetes / med fokus på overvægt
> - Hjemløse i Danmark med fokus på hjemløseavisen 'Hus Forbi'
> - Det danske velfærdssamfund med fokus på statens indtægter
> - Det danske velfærdssamfund i fremtiden
> - Den danske folkeskole med fokus på mobning
> - Det danske arbejdsmarked med fokus på uskrevne regler / med fokus på stress
> - Det danske skattesystem med fokus på proportionalskat
> - Miljø og sundhed med fokus på økologiske fødevarer / med fokus på trafik og forurening
> - Miljø med fokus på vedvarende energi

The same guide lists kinds of topics that schools should **avoid** or **adjust**:
- **Too broad:** for example "Miljø og forurening". The fix is to add a focus, such as "… med fokus på transport".
- **Too concrete or personal:** for example "Vuggestuer i Danmark".
- **Too abstract:** for example "Dansk identitet".
- **Too factual:** for example "Vikingetiden" or "H.C. Andersen". These leave nothing to discuss.
- **Phrased as an opinion question:** for example "Er skattesystemet i Danmark retfærdigt?". These are rewritten as "X med fokus på Y".

#### Your five days of preparation

- **Teaching:** your teacher may teach the topic *in general*, but **you must prepare your presentation on your own** (exam order, annex 1, point 3.35).
- **No help with your presentation:** once you have your topic, you may **not discuss its structure or content with any teacher**, including teachers in the study centre. The only help allowed is a teacher **pointing you to sources**. The examiner may also hand out a reading list (*pensumliste*) of the texts used in class.
- **What SIRI recommends:**
  - Search for information online.
  - Prepare with others who are taking the exam.
  - **Practise out loud** in front of a listener or a mirror, and time yourself.
  - Check that you know the key words, and that your pronunciation and intonation (*prosodi*) support what you say.

#### What you may bring

- **The rule:** your own notes, **no more than one A4 page**, for the presentation (§ 26). SIRI describes this as an outline (*disposition*) plus keywords (*stikord*).
- **No manuscript:** SIRI advises against it. **Reading aloud does not count** in the assessment. If you can't continue without a script, that **lowers your grade**, because there is then nothing to assess for the presentation.

#### The presentation (about 2 minutes)

The official candidate page says:

> *"Det er vigtigt, at din præsentation hverken er kortere eller længere end ca. 2 minutter."*
> (It is important that your presentation is neither shorter nor longer than about 2 minutes.)

- **2–3 points:** SIRI says to choose **only 2–3 points**. More than that leaves no time to explain, give examples or go deeper.
- **Stay general:** keep the presentation general and about society, without (much) personal experience. Your own experience can be useful later in the questions, for example to back up an opinion.
- **No interruptions:** you present alone, without the examiner joining in. Questions start when you finish or when the time is up.
- **What is tested:** whether you can choose relevant sub-points and give a presentation that is easy to follow.

#### The follow-up questions (about 2½ minutes)

The official examiner booklet (May–June 2024) lists these question types:

| Type | What the examiner might say |
|---|---|
| **Clarifying** (*opklarende*) | *"Du sagde, at … Vil du godt forklare det lidt nærmere?"* |
| **Going deeper** (*uddybende*) | *"Du sagde, at … Kan du ikke fortælle lidt mere om det?"* |
| **Explaining and justifying** (*forklaring og begrundelse*) | *"Du sagde, at …, men hvorfor tror du så, at …?"* |
| **Taking a position and arguing** (*stillingtagen og argumentation*) | *"Mener du, at …? Hvorfor?/Hvorfor ikke?"* |

- **Required questions:** the examiner **must** ask questions that require explanation and taking a position.
- **Follow-ups:** if you don't give reasons or you answer beside the point, the examiner **must** ask follow-up questions.
- **The examiner leads:** the examiner chooses the focus, keeps the conversation within the topic and links each question to what you have just said.

SIRI's example for the topic "Christiania med fokus på dens rolle som alternativ boform":

> - Opklarende: *"Du talte om, hvordan beslutningerne på Christiania blev truffet, men jeg forstod det ikke rigtigt. Vil du godt forklare det lidt nærmere?"*
> - Uddybende: *"Du sagde, at der havde været mange forsøg på at lukke Christiania. Kan du ikke fortælle lidt mere om det?"*
> - Ræsonnement: *"Du fortalte, at Christiania blev startet på et ulovligt grundlag. Men hvorfor tror du så, at Christiania eksisterer den dag i dag?"*
> - Stillingtagen: *"Mener du, at Christiania fortsat skal eksistere? Hvorfor?/Hvorfor ikke?"*

### 3.4 Delprøve 2: the unprepared topic (*uforberedt emne*)

#### How it runs

1. **Draw a topic.** Three topics, A, B and C, are on identical slips of paper, and you draw one.
2. **Look at the pictures.** A sheet with **two pictures** (two situations on the same topic) is put in front of you. The examiner says something like:
   > *"Her er der to billeder, som viser nogle situationer, der handler om emnet [emne]. Du har nu 10 sekunder til at overveje emnet, og derefter vil jeg stille dig spørgsmål til én af situationerne."*
3. **First required question.** The examiner picks **one** of the two situations and describes it in one or two sentences. Then comes the **first required question** (*første obligatoriske spørgsmål*). It is about a **concrete** part of the topic and is often a *"Hvorfor tror du, …?"* question.
4. **Follow-ups.** The examiner asks you to clarify, explain or go deeper.
5. **Second required question.** The examiner says that a more general question is coming, then asks the **second required question**. It is about a **broader** part of the topic and is usually about advantages and disadvantages: *"Hvilke fordele og ulemper mener du, der kan være ved …?"*
6. **More follow-ups.** After about 4½ minutes the examiner says *"Tak, så er prøven forbi."*

Good to know:
- **You don't describe the pictures any more.** That task was removed in 2023 (see section 6).
- **Misunderstood questions:** if you don't understand a required question, the examiner **must repeat it**, first with roughly the same words and then possibly rephrased.
- **If you can't answer:** if you can't answer a required question, the examiner makes sure that is clear before moving on.
- **Hard follow-ups are normal.** The examiner asks follow-up questions **even if they think you can't answer them well**, so that it is clear whether you belong at the top, middle or bottom of the scale. Being pushed with harder questions is part of the format, not a bad sign.

#### A real example (May–June 2024, the most recent public set)

**Topic B: *Mangel på arbejdskraft*** (labour shortage). The two pictures were *"Mangel på medarbejdere i sundhedssektoren"* (shortage of health-care staff) and *"Mangel på arbejdskraft i byggebranchen"* (shortage of construction workers).

- **First required question (situation A):**
  > *"Der mangler arbejdskraft i sundhedssektoren, fx sygeplejersker og social- og sundhedsassistenter. Hvorfor tror du, der gør det?"*

  Suggested follow-up: *"Hvilke problemer mener du, der kan være ved, at der mangler sygeplejersker og social- og sundhedsassistenter?"*
- **Second required question:**
  > *"Ved mangel på arbejdskraft kan man fra politisk side vælge at gøre det nemmere for virksomheder at rekruttere udenlandsk arbejdskraft. Hvilke fordele og ulemper mener du, der kan være ved det for samfundet?"*

  Suggested follow-up: *"Mener du, det er i orden at rekruttere udenlandsk arbejdskraft, selvom der er borgere i Danmark, der er arbejdsløse?" "Hvorfor?/Hvorfor ikke?"*

The other two topics in that set were *Støtte til kultur* (state support for culture) and *Pædagogik i folkeskolen* (teaching in the Danish school). SIRI's candidate page also gives *brug af sociale medier* and *jobsøgning og ledighed* as examples.

You can download the pictures and the full examiner booklet for this set, and watch a **video of a passed PD3 oral exam**, from the links in [section 9](#9-sources).

## 4. What is assessed and how the grade works

### 4.1 The assessment criteria

These criteria come from the exam order (BEK 1687/2025, annex 1, points 3.37 and 3.40). Each is given in my translation, followed by the original Danish.

**Delprøve 1:** whether you can give a **prepared presentation of a general topic** and **answer adequately** questions that ask you to **clarify, elaborate, justify and take a position**. It also assesses whether you can **express yourself coherently** in relatively nuanced language that fits the situation, with a relatively high degree of **complexity and correctness**.
> *"…kan give en forberedt præstation af et alment emne, som er på et generelt niveau samt svare adækvat på spørgsmål, der inviterer til opklaring, uddybning, begrundelse og stillingtagen. Det vurderes, om prøvedeltageren kan udtrykke sig sammenhængende i et forholdsvis nuanceret og situationstilpasset sprog med en forholdsvis høj grad af kompleksitet og korrekthed."*

**Delprøve 2:** whether you can **understand** most everyday communication in relatively nuanced, complex language. This is the listening part of the grade. It also covers the same answering and speaking skills as delprøve 1.
> *"…kan forstå hovedparten af almindeligt forekommende kommunikation, hvor der anvendes et forholdsvis nuanceret sprog med en forholdsvis høj grad af kompleksitet. Det vurderes endvidere, om prøvedeltageren kan svare adækvat på spørgsmål, der inviterer til opklaring, uddybning, begrundelse og stillingtagen …"*

SIRI summarises both parts as the ability to **tell, explain, justify, take a position and argue** (*fortælle, forklare, begrunde, tage stilling og argumentere*).

How the grade is set:
- **Holistic:** your grade is one overall judgement, based on detailed criteria.
- **Criteria not public:** the detailed criteria (*bedømmelsesvejledning*) and graded audio examples are on SIRI's assessor training site (du-bedoemmertraening.dk). You need a course key from a language centre to log in, so you may want to ask your teacher about them.

SIRI's checklist says the examiner should **not**:
- correct your mistakes
- "translate" your answers
- signal that things are going well when they aren't, so they say *"tak"* rather than *"fint"* (don't read anything into it)
- ask factual knowledge questions (*"det er ikke en vidensprøve"*: it is not a knowledge test)

The examiner **should** accept pauses and not interrupt you.

### 4.2 Your oral grade

The **7-point scale** (*7-trins-skalaen*), with the official descriptions from BEK 1759/2024:

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **12** | Excellent: meets the goals fully, with no or only a few minor weaknesses |
| **10** | Very good: meets the goals extensively, with some minor weaknesses |
| **7** | Good: meets the goals, with a number of weaknesses |
| **4** | Fair: meets the goals to a lesser degree, with several major weaknesses |
| **02** | Adequate: the minimum acceptable level |
| **00** | Inadequate: not an acceptable level |
| **−3** | Completely unacceptable |

- **When you find out:** you get the grade **straight after the grading**, from the examiner with the censor present.
- **If the examiner and censor disagree:** each gives a grade, and your grade is their **average, rounded to the nearest grade on the scale**. If the average is exactly halfway between two grades, you get the **higher** one if the censor gave the higher grade, and otherwise the lower one. You are never told about a disagreement.
- **Once written down, the grade is final** and binds both the examiner and the censor.

### 4.3 Passing PD3: the oral counts double

PD3 is **passed** if your **average is at least 2.0**, with the oral grade counted twice:

```
average = (reading + writing + 2 × oral) / 4
```

| Reading | Writing | Oral | Average | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 7 | 10 | (4 + 7 + 20) / 4 = **7.8** | Passed (SIRI's own example) |
| 00 | 02 | 4 | (0 + 2 + 8) / 4 = **2.5** | Passed: a good oral makes up for weak written results |
| 4 | 02 | 00 | (4 + 2 + 0) / 4 = **1.5** | **Not passed**: a weak oral pulls the average down |

- **No rounding up:** you can't reach 2.0 by rounding. 1.9 is a fail.
- **No per-part minimum:** PD3 has no minimum grade for each part; only the average counts. This is different from the *Studieprøven*, where every part must be at least 02.

## 5. Practical rules

- **ID (*legitimation*):** bring **original** photo ID to the oral exam as well as the written one. Accepted documents:
  - a passport, residence card (*opholdskort*), alien's passport (*fremmedpas*) or refugee travel document (*konventionspas*) issued in Denmark
  - an EU/EEA or Swiss passport or national ID card
  - a passport from another country

  Copies and photos are not accepted. **A driving licence is never accepted**, and SIRI cannot make an exception for one.
- **Arriving late:** if the delay is reasonably justified, the head of the school can offer you a later slot in the same term.
- **Illness:** with a current doctor's note, the head of the school can let you take the part you missed in a later term.
- **Retaking:** you always register for the written and oral parts **together** and take them in the same term. You **can't retake only the oral** (except because of illness, as above). If you don't pass, you take the whole PD3 again.
- **Confidentiality (since winter 2024):** exam tasks are reused from a closed task bank. You may not take any exam material out of the room, and A2B's slides say sharing the content is illegal. A2B also says you may tell people about the exam from memory.
- **Complaints (*klage*):** a complaint must be written and give your reasons. Send it to the **head of the school that held the exam** within **4 weeks** of getting your result. For an oral exam the outcome can be a **re-exam (*omprøve*)**, possibly later in the same term. A re-exam after a complaint **can give a lower grade**.

## 6. How the oral exam changed, 2016 to 2026

### Background: before 2016

Under BEK 781/2011, the PD3 oral was taken **in pairs** (*parvis*):
1. Each candidate was first examined alone.
2. The two candidates then talked with each other.
3. Finally, the examiner interviewed each candidate again.

This took 30 minutes per pair (20 if alone, 45 with three candidates).

**From 8 March 2013** (BEK 224/2013) the PD3 oral became **individual and 15 minutes long, including grading**, and it has stayed that way. (PD2 is still taken in pairs today, so don't mix them up.)

### 2016: the starting point

The rules in force were BEK 781/2011, amended in 2012 and 2013. SIRI described the exam in its *Vejledning om Prøve i Dansk 3* (September 2016):

- **Delprøve 1:**
  - **Topic:** assigned by your teacher, related to the module 5 curriculum, and given to you 5 weekdays before the oral period, in person or by letter.
  - **Timing:** a **2-minute** presentation, then a **3-minute** interview.
  - **Notes:** **one sheet** of your own notes.
  - **Help:** once the topic was approved, teachers could help with sources but could not help you rehearse the presentation, and the approved topic should not be part of the teaching.
  - **Reuse:** in a large group, one topic could be given to 2–3 candidates.
- **Delprøve 2:** you drew A, B or C and had ~10 seconds to look at two pictures. You then **described both situations yourself (1 minute)**, followed by a **4-minute interview** on the topic.
- **Assessed:** understanding, answering questions, **describing and narrating**, exchanging information, expressing opinions and views, justifying and **persuading** (*påvirke*).
- **Grade:** one oral grade, with the pass rule already average ≥ 2.0 and oral counted double. That rule was already in the 2011 order and has never changed.

### May–June 2018: new exam order (BEK 150/2018)

- **Delprøve 1 topic:** now defined in the rules as a **"general topic at a general level"** (*alment emne på et generelt niveau*), **chosen by the examiner and approved by the head of the school**.
- **Delprøve 1 criteria:** changed from "describe and narrate" to giving a **prepared presentation** and answering questions that ask you to **clarify, elaborate and justify**.
- **Delprøve 2 name:** now called **"situation description and interview"** (*situationsbeskrivelse og interview*).
- **Delprøve 2 rules:** drawing 1 of 3 topics and the ~10 seconds with the pictures were now written into the rules. **Argumentation** was added to the criteria.
- **Notes:** the **one-sheet A4** notes rule was written into the order itself.
- **Timings by 2019:** the November–December 2019 examiner booklet shows:
  - delprøve 1: **2 min** presentation + **2½ min** questions
  - delprøve 2: **1 min** picture description + **4 min** questions
  - each part 5 minutes **including instructions**

  So the delprøve 1 interview had become shorter than in 2016 (3 min). **(Not verified:** the exact term this happened, sometime between September 2016 and November 2019.)

### May–June 2021: BEK 88/2021

- **15-minute frame:** the 15 minutes now explicitly **include instructions and grading**.
- **Teaching:** new rule that once the examiner has chosen your topic, **it may be taught in general**, but you must **prepare your own presentation independently**. In 2016 an approved topic was kept out of the teaching altogether.

### May–June 2023: BEK 347/2023, the biggest change in the period

- **Delprøve 2 redesigned:** it changed from "describe the pictures + interview" to **"an interview on an unprepared topic illustrated by two situations"**.
  - **You no longer describe the pictures.** You get ~10 seconds, then the examiner introduces one situation and asks the **two required questions** with follow-ups for about 4½ minutes.
- **New wording in the criteria:** **"taking a position"** (*stillingtagen*) was added to the delprøve 1 criteria and replaced "argumentation" in the delprøve 2 criteria. (The examiner booklet still uses both words: *stillingtagen og argumentation*.)

### November–December 2024: BEK 1129/2024, exams become confidential

- **Closed task bank:** a 2024 law change (bill L 157, 2023–24) created a **closed task bank** (*lukket opgavebank*). From the **winter 2024 term**, all exam material and answers are **confidential**, reused, and **no longer published**. The latest public PD3 oral material is therefore from **May–June 2024**.
- **ID check:** the 15 minutes now also include the **ID check**.

### 2025

- **1 January 2025:** new exam order BEK 1627/2024, with wording changes only.
- **January 2025:** SIRI updated its guide on choosing topics. The PD1 information moved to a separate guide, and there were minor adjustments.
- **New grading order:** BEK 1759/2024 (in force 1 January 2025, for grades from May–June 2025) replaced the 2007 order. It keeps the **same 7-point scale**. The averaging rule for examiner–censor disagreements was already referred to in the exam orders from 2018 onwards.

### 2026 (current)

- **1 January 2026:** new exam order **BEK 1687/2025**, with wording changes only. For example, "*meddelelse af karakter*" (being told your grade) is now listed as part of the 15 minutes, and the module 5 reference now points to annex 1 of the new education order, BEK 1686/2025.
- **1 March 2026:** **BEK 300/2026**, a technical amendment. It removed § 41(1)–(2) about who grades the oral exams, and renamed a form to *bedømmelsesliste*. For PD3, § 33 still says an examiner and a censor grade together, so **nothing changes for candidates.**
- **October 2026:** SIRI updated its practical guide for exam providers.

### What has not changed

- **Since 2011** (the exam orders):
  - One oral grade on the 7-point scale, which counts double.
  - PD3 is passed at an average of 2.0.
  - A topic for delprøve 1 that relates to DU3 module 5 and is given to you 5 weekdays before the oral period.
- **Since March 2013** (the exam orders): an individual exam with a 15-minute frame, in two parts.
- **Since at least 2016** (SIRI's guides; I found no guide from before 2016):
  - A presentation of about 2 minutes, followed by questions.
  - Drawing 1 of 3 picture topics, with about 10 seconds to look at the pictures.
  - Notes on a single sheet.

## 7. Preparing: official advice and A2B's tips

### From SIRI (official)

- Pick **2–3 points**, aim for **about 2 minutes** and keep it **general and about society**.
- Bring an **outline and keywords**, not a script.
- Practise **out loud** and time yourself. Check your key words, pronunciation and intonation.
- Look for information online, and prepare with others who are taking the exam.

### From A2B's slides

- **Maximum 2 minutes.**
- Start by **telling your plan**, and give the presentation a **beginning, middle and end**.
- Name **3 things** you will talk about, and remember to **cover all 3**.
- Be careful with too many examples, and **don't use examples from your own life**.
- You can **steer the questions by leaving things out** of your presentation.
- **Avoid too many facts.**

### How the two fit together

- **Length:** A2B says "maximum 2 minutes", but SIRI says **neither shorter nor longer than about 2 minutes**. Aim for close to 2 minutes, not well under.
- **Number of points:** SIRI says 2–3 points and A2B says 3. Both work.
- **Steering the questions:** leaving something out *may* lead the examiner to ask about it. But the examiner chooses the focus and **must** ask questions that require reasons and a position, so prepare your **opinions and reasons** on the topic, not just facts.
- **Facts:** "avoid too many facts" fits SIRI's rules. Very factual topics are avoided, and the examiner shouldn't ask knowledge questions.

### A possible 2-minute structure (my suggestion, consistent with both)

| Part | Time | What to say | Useful phrases |
|---|---|---|---|
| Introduction | ~15 sec | Topic and plan | *"Mit emne er … med fokus på … Jeg vil komme ind på tre ting: for det første …, for det andet … og for det tredje …"* |
| Point 1 | ~30 sec | Explain plus one example | *"Det første, jeg vil tale om, er …"* |
| Point 2 | ~30 sec | Explain plus one example | *"Et andet vigtigt punkt er …"* |
| Point 3 | ~30 sec | Explain plus one example | *"Til sidst vil jeg nævne …"* |
| Ending | ~15 sec | A short summary, and your view if you like | *"Sammenfattende kan man sige, at …"* |

For the questions, in both parts, answer and then give a reason and an example:
- *"Jeg tror, at det skyldes, at … For eksempel …"*
- *"En fordel ved det er, at … En ulempe er derimod, at … Alt i alt mener jeg, at …"*
- *"Det kommer an på … På den ene side …, på den anden side …"*
- *"Hvis jeg skal tage stilling, mener jeg, at …, fordi …"*
- To gain time: *"Det er et godt spørgsmål. Lad mig lige tænke."*
- If you didn't understand: *"Undskyld, vil du godt gentage spørgsmålet?"* or *"Mener du, at …?"*

## 10. Addendum: 19 November 2026

These facts were found by the project's `pd3-exam-expert` review on 18 November 2026, which read the primary sources listed below. They haven't been checked again independently. Anything still unconfirmed is marked **(not verified)**.

- **Older material may be used for teaching.**
  - SIRI's practical guide (October 2026, §2.7) says exam material from **summer 2024 and earlier is not confidential and may still be used in teaching**.
  - Everything from the winter 2024 term onwards is confidential and goes into the closed task bank, which is reused.
- **How delprøve 2 material reaches examiners:** per exam day, as sheets of three topics (practical guide §2.8.2).
- **The draw slips:** the three slips must have no numbers or other marks on the side the candidate can see (practical guide §4.3.5).
- **No notes in delprøve 2.** Notes are allowed only for the delprøve 1 presentation (practical guide §4.3.5).
- **The transition before the second required question has no fixed wording.**
  - The May–June 2024 examiner booklet only tells the examiner to announce *"et spørgsmål af en mere overordnet karakter"*.
  - The Nov–Dec 2019 booklet (the old format) had no announcement at all.
- **The situation intro:** before the first required question, the examiner describes the chosen situation in one or two sentences. Each topic in the 2024 booklet has a suggested wording ("Forslag til indledning").
- **A proposal for more topics:** a ministry consultation letter of 9 September 2024 proposed widening the unprepared part from 3 to about 20 questions. Whether and how this was implemented is **(not verified)**.
- **The Huskeliste** for examiners and censors is now dated April 2026.

### Addendum: special arrangements (*særlige prøvevilkår*), added 21 November 2026

This comes from the `pd3-exam-expert` review of 21 November 2026, which checked the facts against retsinformation.dk and SIRI's guide.

- **BEK 1687/2025 § 27:**
  - The school **must** offer special arrangements to candidates with a psychological or physical disability, or similar difficulties, when that's needed for equal conditions. The arrangement must not change the exam's level.
  - The candidate applies in writing to the head of the school **at least 8 weeks before the exam term**, with an expert statement for a disability that isn't obvious. The statement can be up to 2 years old.
  - The head of the school decides. SIRI decides in special cases.
  - Candidates must be told about this option when they register.
- **§ 28:** examples include extra time, technical aids, a helper and an exam in a separate room. The decision must arrive at least 4 weeks before the term.
- **§ 29:** the same grading criteria and level apply.
- **§ 51(4):** the certificate doesn't mention the arrangement.
- **§ 63:** a refusal can be appealed to SIRI within 2 weeks.
- **SIRI practical guide (October 2026) § 3:** extra time is "normalt 25 % af prøvetiden", and more can be granted if needed. **(Not verified):** how extra time is applied within the oral exam.
- **This term:** for winter 2026 the 8-week deadline has probably passed (around mid-September 2026). Ask the school about May–June 2027.
